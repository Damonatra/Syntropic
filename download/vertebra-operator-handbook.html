01 · Arrival

The door & the desk

The enclave is keyed to its operators, not to a shared password. Every dispatch, every acknowledgement, every stage move carries the name of the human who made it — so the door asks who you are before it shows you the desk. There are exactly two keys, and each opens the same room with a different signature on the trail.

The operator checkpoint is the gate page. It lists the accounts the enclave knows: Damon — CEO and Josias — Executive Lead. Choose your row, present your password, and the enclave mints a signed session that lasts thirty days. If your password needs replacing, a rotation re-seals the lock without touching anything else — ask for a fresh one and carry on.

  1. Open the enclave. An unsigned visit lands on the checkpoint automatically.
  2. Choose your account — Damon or Josias. The row highlights; the password field addresses you by name.
  3. Present your password and press enter enclave. Verification is deliberately slow — that is the scrypt lock doing its job.
  4. Land on the Radar, signed in. The rail footer now carries your identity; every action signs to your name.
  5. Leaving? Press Exit in the top bar. Ends the session on this machine; the enclave goes dark behind you.

Once inside, four pieces of furniture matter on day one:

The rail — left edge

Brand, bridge pulse, the six views, watch status, and your identity block (ROLE // NAME). The ‹ · › control collapses it to an icon column — the posture is remembered per browser.

Personas — top bar

The COO / VP Sales / Dir. Inside Sales switcher re-frames the desk for the seat you're speaking from. Your mandate rides beside it; the trail still signs your real name.

The dossier — drawer

One prospect, one drawer: score, evidence, contacts, telemetry, the action log. Open it from any view with a click or Enter; compose cold email inside it with e.

The palette — ⌘K

The command palette jumps anywhere — views, prospects, drafts — without touching the mouse. ? overlays the full keyboard map at any moment.

02 · Cadence

The daily rhythm

The desk is ordered so attention flows downhill: the machine filters all night, the morning sweep spends that work where margin is actually leaking. Run it top-down, in this order — each step feeds the next, and skipping one is how signals rot into noise.

  1. Sign in and read the room. Bridge pulse first — LIVE means the engine is feeding the desk; BRIDGE DOWN means read-only until it returns.
  2. Sweep the Radar. Net-margin leakage, quote-turnaround latency, SPA laggards, manual-quoting share — and each against its 24-hour delta. Drift, not level, is what asks for action.
  3. Clear the alert backlog. High-intent portal hits surface with the company and executive on the card. Open what's hot, acknowledge the rest with an honest reason — real, handled, or false positive. Acks are how the watchtower learns its thresholds.
  4. Work the Strike queue top-down. QLV rank is the machine's bet; walk it. Call the ones worth a voice (c), defer what isn't today (x), snooze the not-yets (s), send cold email where the evidence is strong (e).
  5. Judge the Pipeline. Replies became drafts overnight. Approve what reads true, edit what needs your hand, discard what doesn't. Pending drafts are queued money — the queue should leave the morning near zero.
  6. Move paid work through the Portal. Advance engagements one legal stage each (a), drop fresh audit documents where forensics needs them, and check the ledger for new money.
  7. Close out on Ops. The watchtower deck lists what the engine is watching on your behalf. Quiet is the goal; unacknowledged warnings are tomorrow's first item.

The two-minute version

Radar for drift → acknowledge alerts → Strike until the queue is triaged → Pipeline to zero pending → Portal stages forward → Ops quiet. If the bridge is down, triage from what's already on the desk and let the engine catch up.

03 · Territory

The six views

Six surfaces, one grammar: every view is the same ledger seen from a different altitude. Learn what each is for and the keyboard carries you the rest of the way.

◎ Radar Where margin leaks

For: the executive read. Entry anchor, the four margin KPIs with live 24-hour deltas, the alert backlog, and Module 7 territory corridors with their hot counts.

Work it: read deltas before levels; acknowledge or open alerts; click a corridor to open the Theatre. The strike rail on the right lists who opened the portal in the last hours — those are the warmest names on the page.

▸ Strike Where you spend the morning

For: the QLV-ranked prospect queue. Bands — critical, hot, now — are the machine's bet on who deserves a human next, and the counters on each row show why.

Work it: top-down with the keyboard. Enter opens the dossier — pain signals are verbatim quotes from the prospect's own world, not summaries. Call, defer, snooze, or compose from evidence. Never send prose the evidence doesn't carry.

◈ Theatre Where the map breathes

For: Module 7 spatial clustering — corridors drawn from geocoded HQs, sized by density, colored by heat. It answers "where is the pattern" at a glance.

Work it: zoom and pan, open pulses on live prospects, drop into any cluster to see its members. Use it to brief others; the queue remains the working surface.

▤ Pipeline Where drafts meet judgment

For: triage of inbound replies. The engine classifies intent and drafts the response; the pending queue waits for a human verdict — nothing sends itself.

Work it: cursor down the queue, a to approve a faithful draft, e to edit one that's close, x to discard what should never leave. Space multi-selects for batch moves. Approve only what you'd sign by hand.

◧ Portal Where money becomes work

For: paid engagements. Each client moves one legal stage at a time — paid, intake, forensics, verdict, delivered, sow, closed — with documents and notes bound along the way.

Work it: q opens the queue, a advances the engaged row, the audit dropzone ingests real files for forensics, and c jumps to the ledger — the money room — from anywhere. The verdict stage records the $10,000 guarantee decision honestly: cleared, or not.

▦ Ops Where the engine confesses

For: the watchtower deck. Alerts are re-derived from real rows on every sweep — nothing here is stored opinion — and engine pulses show which watchers last checked in.

Work it: acknowledge fires with a reason. "False positive" rolls up into per-watch hit rates and becomes threshold tuning data; "real" leaves the watch armed. Chronic flappers expose themselves in the fire ledger.

04 · Machinery

The engine loop

The desk is the front of a loop that runs without you — until the moment judgment is required. Knowing where the machine ends and you begin is the whole skill.

  1. Ingest. Prospects arrive from manual entry and engine sweeps, deduped on domain. Filtering and CASL scope are decided before anything earns a stamp.
  2. Forensics. Audit documents are parsed server-side; line items and quote-portal types become structured evidence. Nothing is invented — extraction carries provenance or it doesn't ship.
  3. Score. The QLV model stamps every prospect 0–100 with factors and hooks, banded for the Strike queue. Scores are fingerprints — recomputed, never hand-edited.
  4. Evidence. Pain signals collect the prospect's own words — review complaints, site copy, job postings, reply language — vetoed by operators, hashed into every draft built on them.
  5. Dispatch. Channel A runs automated sequences on the scorable middle; channel B is high-touch, human-led, reserved for the top of the queue. Every send carries a verify report of exactly what left the enclave.
  6. Telemetry. Portal visits, audit views, and pricing reads raise intent heat in real time — this is what makes the strike rail warm and the pager loud.
  7. Inbound. Replies are classified — interested, out of office, objection, not interested — with confidence and rationale attached, then turned into drafts.
  8. The human gate. Drafts wait in the Pipeline for a named operator. Approval is the one step the enclave refuses to automate — the loop's entire integrity lives here.

Where you are irreplaceable

Veto weak pain signals before they teach the drafts bad habits. Approve only email you'd sign by hand. Record verdicts as they are — the guarantee is a contract, and the ledger never forgets. Suppression is permanent: an unsubscribe anywhere silences the address everywhere.

The desk laws

LAW 01

Every action signs to your name.

Calls, acks, verdicts, sends — the trail carries the operator who made it. The enclave has no anonymous hands.

LAW 02

Evidence before prose.

A sentence is only safe to send if it would be false for every other company on the list. Verbatim or nothing.

LAW 03

One-of-one — nothing moves without a human.

The machine scores, drafts, and watches. It never sends. Approval is the gate the loop cannot skip.

LAW 04

The ledger never forgets.

Verify reports, engagement events, operator actions — append-only and honest. Record what is, not what was hoped.
